# Voice First: The Future of Interaction? — NN/g

**Source:** https://www.nngroup.com/articles/voice-first/
**Author:** Nielsen Norman Group
**Captured:** 2026-05-09
**Why it matters here:** UX rules for voice that don't depend on which model is hot this quarter. Voice-output design is its own discipline; ignoring it is how you get a technically-impressive demo nobody uses.

---

## Where voice wins, where it loses

| Voice as input | Voice as output |
|---|---|
| Strong: hands-free, fast for known commands. | Weak: serial, ephemeral, can't be skimmed. |

Screens excel as efficient *output* — users absorb information in parallel. Voice excels as efficient *input*. Don't conflate them.

## Anti-patterns NN/g calls out

- **Missing functionality** — voice agents complete only initial steps and force users to fall back to touch.
- **Poor screen utilization** — Siri-style "I read your message but hide its content while reading" wastes the screen.
- **Limited discoverability** — with thousands of skills available, "users can't be expected to remember the exact name of every skill."

## Design rules they recommend

1. **Sequential numbering for results** — gives the user verbal handles ("the second one").
2. **Immersive, interactive screens** alongside voice — display rich content normally restricted to GUIs.
3. **Suggested commands ambient throughout the interface** — teach affordances by exposure, not memorization.
4. **Don't handicap the screen "in the name of pure voice"** — going voice-only when a screen is right there is "going into a fight with one hand tied behind your back."

## Implications for reading-mate

Voice is the *output* path. The product is fundamentally listening-first. Two consequences:

- **Build for ephemerality.** The user can't scroll back through audio cheaply. Provide chapter/section markers, jump-back-30-seconds, "what was I just listening to" cues.
- **The screen is not the enemy.** Even if the user's eyes are off the device, the controlling surface (a tablet, phone, laptop) should still show structure: where am I in the document, what's coming, what was that link, what was that footnote.

A "sci-fi" reading mate is one that treats voice as the primary medium *and* uses every other modality the device offers — silent visual cues, haptics, ambient state — to compensate for voice's weaknesses, not pretend they don't exist.
